Here is the blunt and practical summary of my review:

### 1. Verdict: **Weak but Defensible (Major Revision)**
The paper is appropriate for an ENIAC 2026 undergraduate track because it honest-reports a negative result (standard DQN with tiny 22-parameter MLP or VQC models fails below the random baseline in 100 episodes) and details simulated VQC timing costs/optimizations. 

However, it is currently **weak** due to:
1. **Abstract/Results Discrepancy**: The abstract/conclusion refers to "VQC L3 fuzzy membership: 321.87 ± 188.69", but the results table and codebase config `cartpole_vqc_l3_distill.yaml` verify that this was actually **L3 raw** (raw normalized features). 
2. **Failure Clarification**: The "collapse below random" (~9.4 vs ~20.6) happens because the policy has learned to output constant actions, which causes the CartPole pole to fall immediately.
3. **Fuzzy Distillation**: The perfect score (500.00) is achieved via **supervised policy cloning** (distillation), which must be clearly separated from DQN learning from reward.

### 4. Suggested Paper Framing
Frame the paper as a **viability, capacity, and cost boundary study**:
* **The representation bottleneck**: Shows that reward-only DQN value learning is unstable and sample-inefficient with tiny models.
* **The capacity proof**: Highlight that the same 22-parameter VQC **can** represent a perfect controller under supervised imitation learning with clean fuzzy membership inputs ([preprocessing.py](file:///home/daniel/artigo-reinforcement-learning-quantico/src/qrl/envs/preprocessing.py#L36)). The bottleneck is thus the sample efficiency of the RL pipeline under extreme parameter constraints, not the expressivity of the VQC architecture.

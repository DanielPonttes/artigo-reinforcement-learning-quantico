\begin{abstract}
Can a 22-parameter quantum circuit replace a neural network in Deep Q-Learning?
We answer this with a rigorous parameter-matched study on CartPole-v1, pitting
a tiny Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) against an equally tiny classical MLP.
The headline is provocative: \textbf{neither agent learns the task under DQN} —
both collapse below a random policy across 5 seeds, even when we widen
exploration and provide engineered fuzzy-membership features. Yet the VQC
\textbf{can} represent a perfect CartPole controller: under supervised
distillation from a heuristic teacher, it achieves the maximum 500-step return
with zero variance across seeds. The gap between these two results isolates a
clean scientific finding — the bottleneck is not representational capacity, but
the DQN reward signal itself at this training budget. We additionally implement
Double DQN and Dueling DQN variants for both architectures, optimize batch
circuit evaluation to cut VQC simulation cost from $44\times$ to $5\times$, and
provide a fully reproducible codebase with 5-seed experiments and 15 verified
references. This is a transparent negative-results study that aims to calibrate
expectations for near-term quantum reinforcement learning.
\end{abstract}
